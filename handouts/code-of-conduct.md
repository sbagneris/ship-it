# Our Class Code

Read this out loud in session 1 and have every student sign it. Keep the signed copies until the course ends.

Signing something is a small piece of theater and it works. It makes the guestbook conversation in session 5 a reminder instead of a new rule.

---

## The Ship It Code

**My website is a real website on the real internet.** Anyone with the link can see it. I'll put things on it I'd be happy for my family, my teacher, and my whole school to see.

**First names only.** No last names — not mine, not anyone's. Not on my page, not in a message I leave on someone else's.

**Nothing that finds me in real life.** No address, no phone number, no email, no school name, no team names, no street I walk down.

**No photos of people.** Not me, not my friends. My dog, my drawing, my Minecraft build — yes.

**I'm kind in other people's guestbooks.** Everyone in this room can see what I write and my name is on it. If I wouldn't say it to their face in front of the class, I don't type it.

**When something breaks, I read the error first.** Then I ask the two people next to me. Then I ask the teacher.

**I help when I'm asked.** I don't take over someone's keyboard. I point.

**I know my teacher can see everything** — every page, every message, and everything I send to the AI. That's not a trick. That's just how it works.

## For the instructor

Two lines do most of the work here.

**"I can see everything"** — said plainly and early — prevents more than any filter. Not delivered as a threat; delivered as a fact about how servers work.

**"If I wouldn't say it to their face in front of the class"** is the one that actually lands with this age group. It converts an abstract rule into a concrete test they can run themselves.
